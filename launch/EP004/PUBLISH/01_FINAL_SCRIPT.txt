THE PROBLEM (2:07–4:07) [S2]
═══════════════════════════════════════
VISUAL: Calm B-roll · kinetic type · lower-thirds
ON-SCREEN: Fluency ≠ authenticity

The problem isn't that AI writes.
The problem is we treat the first draft as the final voice.

Fluency tricks us.
If it sounds smooth, we assume it's ready.
Smooth is not the same as true.

So let's separate three things:

One — Drafting is labour AI can share.
Two — Buzzwords are camouflage, not professionalism.
Three — Your specific details are the trust signal.

If your value is only "I can generate text,"
yes, that layer is crowded.

If your value includes judgment, relationship, and lived context —
your edit is the product.

THE SKILL (4:07–6:07) [S3]
═══════════════════════════════════════
VISUAL: Title card THE HUMAN EDIT LOOP · Pathway 01/02/03 · screen demo
ON-SCREEN: Draft · Strip · Stamp
DEMO: AI email draft → strike buzzwords → stamp SA voice detail → final send-ready

Here's the skill.
Framework name: THE HUMAN EDIT LOOP

Step 1 — Draft.
Let AI give you a first version.
Ask for structure, not personality.
Example: "Draft a short email confirming Thursday 10:00, warm professional South African English, no buzzwords."

Step 2 — Strip.
Delete empty phrases.
Kill: leverage, synergy, delve, tapestry, "I hope this finds you well" if you never say that.
Cut anything you wouldn't say out loud to a colleague in Braamfontein or Polokwane.

Step 3 — Stamp.
Add one true detail only you would know:
the project name, the child's class, the exact invoice number, the joke you share, the real reason for the delay.
Then read it aloud.
If your mouth trips, your reader will too.

That is the Human Edit Loop.
Draft. Strip. Stamp.
You stop sounding like a template —
and you start sending work that carries your name honestly.
