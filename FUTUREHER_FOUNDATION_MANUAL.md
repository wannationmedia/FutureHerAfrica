## 5. Editorial Standards

FutureHer content is curriculum with a broadcast layer — not content for content’s sake.

### 5.1 Editorial law

Every major lesson must deliver:

1. **One new idea** — a reframing she can remember  
2. **One practical skill** — something she can do  
3. **One action today** — a next step under 30 minutes  

### 5.2 Content quality bar

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | Hard claims fact-checked or softened. No fake stats. No guaranteed money outcomes. |
| **Clarity** | Plain language. Jargon translated. One job per piece. |
| **Evidence** | Prefer sources, demonstrated methods, or clearly labelled experience. Speculation must be marked. |
| **SA relevance** | Cost, platforms, work culture, language, and constraints must fit South African reality. |
| **Dignity** | No mocking of struggle. Fear may be named; the viewer is never the punchline. |
| **Longevity** | Ask: would we proudly publish this five years from now? |
| **Series logic** | Episodes belong to a curriculum family, not only a calendar gap. |

### 5.3 Structure standard (default lesson arc)

`Hook → Context → Framework → Demo → Action → Recap`

- Cold open with the idea in the first 15–30 seconds  
- Hold shots long enough to breathe  
- Captions always on for mobile  
- Psychology supports; skill leads  

### 5.4 Forbidden editorial patterns

- Motivation without a skill  
- Trend chasing as curriculum  
- US-generic advice with a South African flag sticker  
- Diagnosis theatre (“you have trauma”) as entertainment  
- Outrage, controversy farming, or shame hooks  
- Collage chaos, bait thumbnails, deceptive titles  
- Stacking subscribe + download + join + buy in one lesson  

### 5.5 Packaging honesty

**Promise = delivery.**  
Title and thumbnail must be true in the first 30 seconds of the lesson. Curiosity is allowed. Deception is not.

### 5.6 Voice & language

- SA English natural; calm mentor cadence  
- Sentence case preferred; restrained titles  
- Brand microcopy stays consistent: *Welcome forward.* / *Ready for what’s next.*  
- Mixed-language speech in community is welcomed; never shamed  

### 5.7 Series identity (broadcast families)

Series are topic rooms under one brand. Wordmark is always **FutureHer**; series name is secondary (`FutureHer · Money`).

Examples of series gravity: Limpopo · Money · AI · Career · Business · Life · Relationships · Stories · Student · Entrepreneur · Rural · Digital — each with one purpose and one visual accent from the brand system (Ink, Brass, Clay, Lagoon, Stone).

Full creative execution lives in `FUTUREHER_BRAND_DESIGN_STUDIO.md` (Phase 6). Messaging and name clearance: `FUTUREHER_BRAND_GUIDELINES.md`.

## 6. Ethical Guidelines

Ethics are not a marketing theme. They are operating constraints.

### 6.1 Non-negotiable ethical laws

1. **Dignity first.** Never exploit insecurity for engagement.  
2. **Truth in claims.** Soften what you cannot verify.  
3. **No harm instruction.** No dangerous, illegal, or exploitative how-tos.  
4. **No hustle-scam adjacency.** No fake scarcity, miracle income, or MLM energy.  
5. **Money caution.** High bar for fintech, investing, and income claims. Prefer regulated, transparent, SA-relevant options.  
6. **AI honesty.** Teach capability and limits. No fearmongering; no magic promises.  
7. **Disclosure.** Every affiliate, sponsor, and gifted partnership is disclosed clearly.  
8. **Consent.** Personal stories and member features require permission.  
9. **Privacy.** Protect member data, voice notes, and private shares.  
10. **Say no.** Audience trust outranks any fee.

### 6.2 Recommendation ethics

Recommend only what:

- we would use, have tested, or can evaluate fairly;  
- fits the lesson (teach first, recommend second);  
- does not prey on desperation;  
- can be defended five years later.

**Hard bans:** crypto hype pumps, payday-adjacent products, body insecurity sells, get-rich-in-7-days claims, undisclosed paid promotion.

### 6.3 Boundaries of advice

| Domain | FutureHer may | FutureHer must not |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Teach budgeting, systems, literacy, questions to ask | Give personalised investment advice or guarantee returns |
| Career | Teach positioning, communication, job-search systems | Promise employment outcomes |
| AI / tech | Teach tools, prompts, workflows, safety | Claim tool permanence or replace professional counsel |
| Mental / relational | Teach communication skills and calm frameworks | Diagnose, treat, or replace therapy/medical care |
| Legal | Point to principles and official resources | Practise law |

### 6.4 Partner & sponsor ethics

| Accept when | Reject when |
|---|---|
| Product genuinely helps the audience | Fee requires deceptive packaging |
| Disclosure is allowed and prominent | Partner sells fear, shame, or fake wealth |
| Partnership is few and deep | Scatter spam / weekly affiliate noise |
| Creative control protects brand voice | Brief demands hype cadence or banned claims |

### 6.5 AI use ethics (internal)

- AI accelerates research, drafts, SEO options, and recycling.  
- Founder (or appointed editor) owns truth, voice, and ethics.  
- Never publish unverified AI claims as fact.  
- Never use AI to fabricate member stories, testimonials, or credentials.

## 8. Accessibility Standards

Accessibility is trust infrastructure. Premium does not mean inaccessible.

### 8.1 Design for the real audience

Default assumptions:

- Phone-first viewing  
- Variable data and bandwidth  
- Mixed literacy and language comfort  
- Noisy environments; phone speaker audio  
- Limited time (busy women, caregivers, workers, students)

### 8.2 Non-negotiable accessibility baseline

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Captions** | Readable captions on every video; English always |
| **Audio** | Clear on phone speaker; no music drowning voice |
| **Thumbnails / text** | Readable at stamp size; max 4–6 words visible |
| **Lesson design** | One idea / one skill / one action — cognitive accessibility |
| **Actions** | Today’s action under 30 minutes; phone-possible when claimed |
| **Documents** | Worksheets readable on phone; clear hierarchy; high contrast |
| **Language** | Plain language; jargon translated |
| **Rural / low-bandwidth** | Prefer teachable principles over heavy unoptimised media when possible; offer text/checklist companions |

### 8.3 Progressive access layers

Do not delay shipping readiness while waiting for perfect access. Expand layers over time:

| Layer | What ships |
|---|---|
| L0 | Clear SA English teaching + on-screen clarity |
| L1 | English captions always |
| L2 | Priority African-language subtitles on high-demand evergreen |
| L3 | Community voice notes in local languages |
| L4 | Partial dubs / bilingual segments where demand is proven |
| L5 | Full local-language remakes for flagship core topics only |

### 8.4 Inclusive representation

- Show South African and African contexts as normal, not exotic.  
- Avoid stock clichés that erase rural, township, campus, and home-office realities.  
- Never imply that future-ready only looks urban, wealthy, or Western.

### 8.5 Accessibility QC (publish gate)

Before publish, confirm:

- Captions readable on phone  
- Audio clear on phone speaker  
- Thumbnail readable at small size  
- Action is realistically doable under stated constraints

## 9. Multilingual Strategy

FutureHer is a **multilingual educational media company**.  
The mark stays **FutureHer**. Access expands through language layers.

### 9.1 Principle

> Language is access. Access is trust. Trust is the brand.

We do not treat English as moral superiority.  
We do not delay the English master until every language is perfect.  
We deepen language access where demand, harm-prevention, and evergreen value are highest.

### 9.2 Language tags

| Code | Language | Strategic role |
|---|---|---|
| `EN` | English | Primary production language |
| `NSO` | Sepedi | Limpopo gravity |
| `VE` | Tshivenda | Limpopo gravity |
| `TS` | Xitsonga | Limpopo gravity |
| `ZU` | isiZulu | National scale |
| `SS` | siSwati | National / regional scale |
| `AF` | Afrikaans | National access layer (add where demand warrants; not Limpopo-first) |
| `XH` | isiXhosa | National scale (phase after ZU demand proven) |

Every major topic should eventually carry: production language, subtitle priority, community welcome languages, and (rarely) dub priority.

### 9.3 Rollout order

1. **EN master** ships with EN captions.  
2. **Limpopo-first subtitles** (NSO / VE / TS) on top evergreen and high local-demand lessons.  
3. **National subtitles** (ZU / SS) as reach expands.  
4. **Community voice notes** in local languages weekly in Limpopo Circle.  
5. **Dubs / remakes** only for flagship topics with proven retention and demand.

### 9.4 Translate-first themes

Prioritise translation where stakes and reach compound:

1. Online scams & cyber safety  
2. Phone-first AI / digital literacy  
3. Money basics (salary, saving, data/airtime reality)  
4. Job hunting on a phone  
5. Limpopo opportunities & local skills  
6. Confidence / communication scripts  
7. Caregiving + skills return  
8. Student study systems  

### 9.5 Community language practice

- WhatsApp Limpopo Circle: English + Sepedi / Tshivenda / Xitsonga welcome  
- Never shame mixed-language speech  
- Brand microcopy remains English for consistency  
- Local greetings welcome in community spaces  

### 9.6 Translation priority formula

```
Translate Priority =
  (Audience demand signals × 3)
+ (Educational impact / life-stakes × 3)
+ (Evergreen score × 2)
+ (Phone-first rural reach × 2)
+ (Safety / scam prevention value × 2)
+ (Series binge potential × 1)
− (High jargon / fragile tool-UI dependency)
```

## 11. Decision Framework

Whenever FutureHer creates content — or launches a product, partnership, campaign, or community ritual — ask:

| # | Question | If “no” |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Does this solve a real problem?** | Do not make it. Research the Problem Bank. |
| 2 | **Is it evidence-based?** | Verify, soften, or cut the claim. |
| 3 | **Can someone use this today?** | Add a concrete action or kill the piece. |
| 4 | **Does it respect the audience?** | Rewrite tone, framing, or packaging. |
| 5 | **Would we proudly publish this five years from now?** | Trend-only ideas get rejected or reduced to packaging for evergreen value. |

### Secondary checks (after the five pass)

- Does this obey Core Principles (§0)?  
- Does this keep the Brand Promise (§4)?  
- Does this pass Ethical Guidelines (§6)?  
- Is the primary CTA singular and earned?  
- Which engine does this strengthen — and does it harm another?

### Decision owners

| Decision class | Default owner |
|---|---|
| Editorial / ethics / brand | Founder |
| Process / templates / weekly ship | Founder as COO seat (see Operating System) |
| Community moderation severity | Founder or appointed moderator using §7 |
| Partner acceptance | Founder using §6.4 |

**Tie-breaker:** Protect trust.

## 12. The FutureHer Operating Model — Six Engines

FutureHer runs on six engines.  
Each is an **independent system** with its own job, inputs, outputs, and success metrics.  
Together they form the company.

```
┌─────────────┐
│   BRAND     │  Identity, trust, creative law
└──────┬──────┘
       │
┌──────▼──────┐     ┌─────────────┐
│  CONTENT    │────▶│  COMMUNITY  │
└──────┬──────┘     └──────┬──────┘
       │                   │
┌──────▼──────┐     ┌──────▼──────┐
│ EDUCATION   │◀───▶│  COMMERCE   │
└──────┬──────┘     └──────┬──────┘
       │                   │
       └─────────┬─────────┘
                 ▼
          ┌─────────────┐
          │   GROWTH    │  Ethical distribution & compounding reach
          └─────────────┘
```

**Systems rule:** Content without Community is broadcast. Community without Education is a chat group. Education without Commerce is fragile. Commerce without Brand/Ethics is extraction. Growth without the others is noise.
